The interaction cross section for an specific interaction in [[The Standard Model of Particle Physics]] is calculated by combining the interaction rate found using [[Fermi's Golden Rule]] with the incident flux.
Thus, the interaction cross section gives the probability of a decay occurring, defined heuristically by
$$
\sigma = \frac{\text{number of interactions per unit time per target}}{\text{incident flux of particles}}
$$
For the transition rate $\Gamma_{fi}$ found in given colliding initial particles $a$ and $b$, the cross section is related to fluxes via the incident particle velocities:
$$
\sigma = \frac{\Gamma_{fi}}{v_a + v_b}
$$
Since the cross section should be Lorentz Invariant, it clear that the transition rate is not Lorentz Invariant to account for spatial dilations of the flux. This is because the time of the decay rate is subject to temporal dilation and thus relative.

For a 2 body to 2 body scattering process, the cross section in the center of mass frame is found by integrating the matrix element over the phase space:
$$
\sigma = \frac{1}{64\pi^2s}\frac{p_f^*}{p_i^*}\int|\mathcal{M}_{fi}|^2 d \Omega^*
$$
This comes from a formula for the differential cross section for a 2 body to 2 body interaction, which is similarly given by
$$
\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega} = \frac{S|\mathcal{M}_{fi}|^2}{64\pi^2s}\frac{p_f^*}{p_i^*}
$$
where the symmetry factor $S$ is $1$ if the final particles are different and $1/2!$ if they are identical.
